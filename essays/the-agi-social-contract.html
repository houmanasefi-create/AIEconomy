The AGI Social Contract

A social contract is the deep agreement about why people accept the system around them. In the age of AGI, that agreement cannot rest only on jobs, wages, and consumer choice. If intelligence changes production, power, and opportunity, the terms of belonging must change too.

The old contract offered a path: learn, work, earn, consume, retire. It excluded many and disappointed many, but it gave society a story about effort and reward. AGI weakens that story when output can grow without requiring the same volume of human labor.

A new contract must begin with shared claims on automated abundance. People should not be treated as surplus because machines can perform more tasks. The purpose of an economy is not to keep humans busy for its own sake. It is to sustain human life, freedom, development, and dignity.

This does not mean a world without responsibility. A durable AGI contract must connect rights with contribution, but contribution has to be broader than wage labor. Care, learning, civic service, creativity, local stewardship, and human presence all matter even when markets fail to price them well.

The contract must also govern power. AGI cannot be allowed to become a private sovereign layer above society. Systems that shape rights, opportunities, public speech, security, and access to services need democratic oversight, auditability, and meaningful appeal.

The material base matters. Public compute, universal AI access, education, healthcare, housing, and transition income are not separate welfare debates. They are the infrastructure of legitimacy in a world where intelligence is a productive force.

A system thinker sees the social contract as a stabilizer. If productivity rises but dignity, agency, and security fall, the system becomes politically unstable. If gains are shared and power remains contestable, AGI can expand the circle of freedom.

The AGI social contract should be simple in spirit: no person becomes disposable because intelligence became cheap; no institution gains unaccountable power because intelligence became powerful; and the surplus created by machine cognition must help build a society in which humans can still meaningfully choose their lives.
