AI and Fair Competition

Fair competition in AI is not about making every company equal. It is about preventing the essential pathways of intelligence from being locked before society understands what has been locked. When compute, data, talent, cloud distribution, and user access gather in the same hands, markets can appear open while the system is already closed.

AI markets do not concentrate only because one product is better. They concentrate because the stack reinforces itself. More users create more data. More data improves models. Better models attract more users. More revenue buys more compute. More compute widens the lead. This loop can become a private gravity well.

Traditional competition policy often waits for price harm. That lens is too narrow for AI. The early harm may be dependency, reduced contestability, invisible bundling, degraded autonomy, or the disappearance of independent builders who never get enough access to compete. By the time prices reveal the problem, the structure may already be permanent.

The point is not to punish scale. Some scale is necessary for frontier systems. The point is to separate scale that serves capability from scale that blocks future competition. A society can allow large models while still refusing exclusive choke points around compute, distribution, identity, payments, app stores, data access, and public procurement.

Open source helps, but it is not a complete answer. A model without affordable inference, safe hosting, high-quality data, and trusted distribution is not a competitor. Openness at one layer can coexist with enclosure at another. System power hides in the layer that everyone else must pass through.

Fair competition requires public rules for access. Interoperability, portability, auditing rights, cloud neutrality, procurement diversity, and anti-bundling rules matter because they keep the system from hardening around a few private control points. These rules do not create innovation from above. They keep innovation from being suffocated below.

The AI economy will not stay competitive by nostalgia for small firms. It will stay competitive if new entrants can reach the inputs that matter and if users can leave without losing their lives to a platform. Freedom to build and freedom to exit are the two sides of the same market design.

Competition is a form of system health. It keeps intelligence plural, prevents single points of failure, and gives society alternatives when dominant actors make mistakes. In AI, fairness is not sentiment. It is resilience.
