The AGI Economy

The AGI economy begins when intelligence becomes a general input rather than a specialized service. At that point, every sector is forced to ask which parts of its work were limited by cognition, coordination, search, and judgment rather than by physical materials alone.

AGI should not be imagined as a single event that instantly replaces the economy. It is better understood as a new layer in the production system. Like electricity, it spreads unevenly, combines with older infrastructure, and reveals bottlenecks that were previously hidden.

The first effect is acceleration. Research, design, administration, code, logistics, finance, media, and education all become faster where tasks can be represented and delegated. But acceleration is not the same as progress. A bad system accelerated becomes a faster bad system.

The second effect is revaluation. Scarcity moves away from routine cognition and toward compute, energy, data, trust, legitimacy, physical assets, human attention, and institutional judgment. Value follows the remaining bottlenecks. Those who control them will try to convert temporary advantage into permanent power.

The third effect is distributional stress. If AGI raises output while concentrating ownership, the economy can grow and fracture at the same time. The central political issue will not be whether AGI is productive. It will be who has claims on the productive base.

AGI also changes the state. Governments will need intelligence capacity to regulate, deliver services, defend institutions, and understand systems moving at machine speed. A state without AI capacity will govern through delay and dependency.

A system thinker treats AGI as a change in operating conditions. It alters feedback loops, compresses time, shifts bargaining power, and tests institutions. The economy will not be transformed by capability alone, but by the way capability enters ownership, law, infrastructure, and culture.

The AGI economy is not predetermined. It can become a rent machine, a public capacity engine, a surveillance order, or a broad expansion of human agency. The technology opens the phase space. Institutions choose the attractor.
