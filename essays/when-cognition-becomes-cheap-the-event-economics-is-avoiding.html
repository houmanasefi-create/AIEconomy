When Cognition Becomes Cheap

Economics was built around scarcity, and for good reason. Land, labor, capital, time, and knowledge were limited. AI does not end scarcity. It makes one historic scarcity cheaper: usable cognition. That is the event many economic conversations still avoid looking at directly.

Cognition is not the same as wisdom, legitimacy, or truth. It is the ability to process, generate, compare, summarize, plan, and solve. When that ability becomes widely available through machines, the economy changes because many costs were really cognition costs hiding under other names.

Management was partly cognition. Compliance was partly cognition. Software was partly cognition. Research, design, customer service, translation, analysis, tutoring, marketing, and bureaucracy were all shaped by the price of thinking at scale. Cheap cognition touches all of them.

The mistake is to ask only which jobs disappear. The deeper question is which coordination problems become solvable, which institutions lose their justification, which bottlenecks become visible, and which new bottlenecks emerge. Cheap cognition is a system shock, not a labor-market headline.

When a core input becomes cheap, society often wastes time defending the old price. Scribes did not define literacy forever. Human calculators did not define computation forever. The humane task is not to preserve every old scarcity, but to preserve human agency through the transition.

Cheap cognition will make some things abundant and others more precious. Trust, attention, embodied care, physical infrastructure, judgment, and shared reality may become harder to maintain. The economy will not be measured only by how much intelligence it can generate, but by whether it can orient that intelligence toward coherent ends.

A system thinker watches for second-order effects. If everyone can produce content, curation becomes power. If everyone can code, distribution becomes power. If everyone can analyze, trusted authority becomes power. Abundance at one layer concentrates scarcity at another.

The avoided event is already underway. Cognition is moving from a human bottleneck into a machine layer. The societies that do best will not deny this. They will redesign education, ownership, governance, and public capacity around the new price of thought.
